## Sequence

1. Geometry: four 40 ns deterministic runs. Compare the 7 micrometre box
   with a commensurate 10.5 micrometre box; split the 20 nm film into two
   10 nm cells; then halve all three original cell dimensions. B0, physical
   pump, material constants, and continuous initial profile remain fixed.
   The final grid is 2800 x 16 x 2, with a 0.1 ps integration step.
   The q/2 frequency must change by at most 3 MHz and its readable
   fixed-window growth rate by at most 5%. These are prospective numerical
   accuracy budgets, not statistical confidence intervals. There is no
   retuning of B0 to hide discretization-induced detuning.
2. Ensemble: twelve NEW independent random-phase seeds, each in MEL, pump-off,
   and MR-only channels, giving 36 separate 40 ns runs. Initial amplitudes
   are identical across seeds and channels. The endpoint is the complex
   +q/2, positive-3-GHz amplitude in the predeclared 30-40 ns window.
   Each term in the ensemble statistic is one run, never an overlapping
   temporal segment. The amplitude-preserving null uses 100,000 independent
   pair-phase surrogates. C = |sum(a_j^2)| / sum(|a_j|^2) is appropriate to
   the degenerate q/2 channel. It is not evidence for two distinct peaks.
   The primary criterion is conditional exceedance below 0.01 together with
   a positive lower bound of the paired-bootstrap MEL-minus-OFF contrast.
   All three channel results are reported, including negative results.
3. Threshold: direct integrations at strain 0, 2e-5, and 5e-5, seeded with
   the predicted physical q/2-sector Floquet eigenvector. Runtime is fixed
   before integration from two predicted amplitude e-folding times, rounded
   upward to 20 ns and capped at 600 ns. The unchanged estimator must read
   the initial linear rate, reproduce its sign, and agree to 5%. HELD is
   not converted into a negative point. A direct bracket and a refined
   Floquet zero are reported separately. THIS IS A q/2-SECTOR THRESHOLD,
   NOT A PROVEN GLOBAL MINIMUM OVER ALL SPATIAL MODES.
4. Thermal signal: twelve independently prepared 300 K initial states;
   each is used for matched pump-on/off 40 ns observations with independent
   subsequent noise streams. Brown noise is generated by the actual engine.
   Preparation uses alpha=0.05 for 10 ns to approach the same assumed
   equilibrium distribution faster; observation restores alpha=0.0005.
   No preparation growth rate is used as a physical result. Preparation
   stationarity diagnostics must be examined before an equilibrium claim.
   The thermal integration step is 0.2 ps. This is separate from the
   zero-temperature spatial convergence test and is not a certification of
   convergence of every stochastic observable.

No step certifies all of the submitted paper's claims. In particular, the
deterministic phase ensemble is conditional on its initial-state distribution,
and does not replace a thermal ensemble or an experimental noise model.

Before any thermal production data were generated, the same +q/2, positive
3 GHz, 30-40 ns phase endpoint was also assigned to the twelve thermal
MEL/OFF pairs. Run `python thermal_phase_followup.py` after thermal completion.
It uses the frozen estimator and amplitude-preserving null, with independent
thermal realizations as its sampling units, and refuses missing/foreign data.
It performs no additional window, frequency, or mode selection. Its
`thermal_phase.json` result remains distinct from the zero-temperature
initial-phase ensemble and from an experimental optical phase measurement.

## Thermal Checkpoints

The old harness correctly refuses incomplete thermal checkpoints without RNG
restoration. The new thermal runner does not bypass that guard: it implements
a separately declared block-stream protocol. Every 2 ns block receives its
own reproducible seed, derived from the run seed and block index. A checkpoint
saves the magnetization, absolute clock, samples, next block, and full
source/build/input identity. An interrupted block is rerun from its saved
boundary using the same stream.

The field assignment that normalizes magnetization is performed exactly once
at each block boundary in both uninterrupted and resumed execution. The first
preflight exposed an extra-normalization difference of 1.3644e-7 in my;
the matching-boundary implementation produced zero difference without relaxing
the fixed 1e-7 tolerance. The failed artifacts are retained in
`validation_preflight_roundtrip_v1`. Pre-production intermediate artifacts are
retained in `validation_preflight_v2`; they are not adopted as current caches.

Preflight also checks the positive temporal branch, a specified synthetic
ensemble's detection power, the engine noise variance against its SI
fluctuation-dissipation expression, and refusal of a changed thermal seed.
See the current `validation/preflight.json` for actual measurements and hashes.

## Conditional Detection

The numerical observable is my spectral power in positive-k bins 5,6,7,
2.5-3.5 GHz, over 20-40 ns. Every bin is counted once and k=0 is excluded.
It is not automatically the optical BLS observable: optical polarization,
spatial collection, momentum acceptance, frequency response, and efficiency
would need calibration before converting it to a measured count rate.

For an assumed linear transfer selecting that band, let r be the thermal
pump-on/off mean-power ratio, M the detected off-magnon count per exposure,
and b the optical-background/off-magnon count ratio. With independent
Poisson on/off counts and equal exposure lengths, the shot-noise-only limit is

    SNR = (r - 1) sqrt(M / (r + 1 + 2b)).

The output tabulates the required M for SNR=5 at declared hypothetical b,
not an actual detector performance. Exposure time is M/R_off; R_off is
unknown. Pulse-to-pulse thermal intensity fluctuations add a separate variance
v*M^2, giving sqrt(N)*(r-1)*M/sqrt((r+1+2b)*M+v*M^2) for N independent pulses.
Independent experimental exposures use Var(on)+Var(off). The variance reduction
from the simulation's shared initial states is recorded separately, not used
to assume experimental covariance. Bootstrap uncertainty of r is reported.

The thermal-noise implementation and fixed-step scaling were checked against
the local engine and the primary MuMax implementation:
https://github.com/mumax/3/blob/master/engine/temperature.go .
These are implementation checks, not validation of a specific optical setup.
